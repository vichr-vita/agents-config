# Method

## Mental contrasting

A wish names a skill, proficiency, or other result. The outcome explores what that achievement would mean and feel like. Next comes an honest account of likely difficulties, including the user's reactions to them. Planning turns those difficulties into if-then responses.

Imagining success alone can leave the practical difficulty untouched. Listing difficulties alone can discourage action. Mental contrasting brings the desired future and the present obstacle together, then connects that obstacle with a response. Preserve this sequence rather than replacing it with ordinary task decomposition.

Record the answers, then reread and revise them when circumstances or motivation change. Do not impose a fixed exercise duration or promise particular results.

The proposed connection to the anterior midcingulate cortex is speculative. Do not claim WOOP activates a particular brain region, rewires the brain in a proven way, or reliably induces a particular neurochemical state.

## Core exercise

Preserve the order of the four steps and have the user imagine the outcome and obstacle. Choose one main inner obstacle with one response for the core exercise. A broader obstacle inventory can inform supporting tasks afterward.

An inner obstacle is a pattern the user recognizes, not an agent's diagnosis. External constraints remain real. If the wish is beyond the user's influence, help them revise it, wait, or let it go. Do not pressure them to persist with an impossible plan.

Mental contrasting links commitment to expectations of success. It is a tool for choosing feasible commitments, not for guaranteeing every wish.

## Adaptation for this skill

Vikunja projects, minimum versions of habits, workload estimates, explicit completion criteria, and review scheduling are practical choices for this coaching workflow. They are not additional WOOP stages. Keep them after the mental exercise.

The user's chosen default is to keep the coaching document in the Vikunja project description. Repeating task descriptions hold each habit's working instructions. No separate local file or public Page is required.
