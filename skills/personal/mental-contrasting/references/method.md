# Method and sources

## The book

This skill draws on Andrew D. Huberman's *Protocols: An Operating Manual for the Human Body*, Learning Protocol 2, "Use Mental Contrasting to Achieve a 'State of Urgency' and Reach Your Goals," printed pages 465 to 476. The user supplied the EPUB. The section is `index_split_055.html`, with supporting endnotes 159, 161, 162, 167, and 168 in `index_split_077.html`.

The notes below paraphrase that section. The book and its text are not bundled with this skill, and the EPUB is not needed to use it.

The book distinguishes a concrete wish from the experience of achieving it. The wish names a skill, proficiency, or other result. The outcome explores what that achievement would mean and feel like. Next comes an honest account of likely difficulties, including the user's reactions to them. Planning turns those difficulties into if-then responses.

Imagining success alone can leave the practical difficulty untouched. Listing difficulties alone can discourage action. Mental contrasting brings the desired future and the present obstacle together, then connects that obstacle with a response. Preserve this sequence rather than replacing it with ordinary task decomposition.

Huberman recommends recording the answers. His account describes an initial written exercise of about ten minutes, followed by rereading and revision when circumstances or motivation change. Treat those durations and his personal results as examples, not requirements or promises.

The chapter's proposed connection to the anterior midcingulate cortex is explicitly speculative. Do not claim WOOP activates a particular brain region, rewires the brain in a proven way, or reliably induces a particular neurochemical state.

## Clarifications from the method's creators

The [official WOOP practice guide](https://woopmylife.org/en/practice) emphasizes imagining, preserving the order of the four steps, and choosing one main inner obstacle with one response. Use that focus for the core exercise. The book's broader obstacle inventory can inform supporting tasks afterward.

An inner obstacle is a pattern the user recognizes, not an agent's diagnosis. External constraints remain real. If the wish is beyond the user's influence, help them revise it, wait, or let it go. Do not pressure them to persist with an impossible plan.

In the [2009 energization study](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167208330856), mental contrasting linked commitment to expectations of success. It is a tool for choosing feasible commitments, not for guaranteeing every wish. The book also cites [Kappes and colleagues' 2012 study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2012.02.002) on linking obstacles with instrumental behavior.

## Adaptation for this skill

Vikunja projects, minimum versions of habits, workload estimates, explicit completion criteria, and review scheduling are practical choices for this coaching workflow. They are not additional WOOP stages or claims from the book. Keep them after the mental exercise.

The user's chosen default is to keep the coaching document in the Vikunja project description. Repeating task descriptions hold each habit's working instructions. No separate local file or public Page is required.
